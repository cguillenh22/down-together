## What Is the DS-CDP

The Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development (NICHD), through the NIH's INCLUDE Project, has launched the **Down Syndrome Cohort Development Program (DS-CDP)**, a long-term study designed to observe and track health data of people with Down syndrome from birth through adulthood. <cite index="1-1">The NIH INCLUDE Project launched a new long-term study, called the Down Syndrome Cohort Development Program, to observe and track health data of people with Down syndrome from birth to adulthood.</cite>

## Program Goals

The initiative aims to substantially expand the data available for research. <cite index="1-6">The DS-CDP aims to engage and enroll thousands of individuals with Down syndrome, including those who have been historically underrepresented in Down syndrome research.</cite> According to project leaders, <cite index="1-7">the goal of the new program is to deepen understanding of Down syndrome and accelerate knowledge of health conditions that more commonly affect those with Down syndrome, with participants sharing biological samples, health information, and other data that will be made anonymous and available to the research community.</cite>

Organizers stress the balance between scientific usefulness and data protection: <cite index="1-8">the DS-CDP aims to provide representative, meaningful, and timely data to investigators and the entire Down syndrome community while ensuring participant privacy.</cite>

## Why It Matters for the Community

Longitudinal cohorts like this one are essential because they allow scientists to study, with large-scale real-world data, how conditions that occur more frequently in people with Down syndrome (cardiac, cognitive, autoimmune, among others) evolve over a lifetime — something one-time studies cannot capture. NIH leadership underscored the importance of keeping the community central to the process: <cite index="1-9">"NIH is committed to keeping the Down syndrome community at the heart of this inclusive research program,"</cite> said Gary H. Gibbons, INCLUDE Co-Chair.

## How to Get Involved

The NIH is actively inviting families, self-advocates, scientists, and clinicians to take part in upcoming requests for information and listening sessions. Families interested in research can also register with DS-Connect, the NIH's national registry, to receive updates about studies like this one as enrollment opens. As always, any decision about participating in clinical or medical studies should be discussed with the healthcare team supporting the person with Down syndrome.
