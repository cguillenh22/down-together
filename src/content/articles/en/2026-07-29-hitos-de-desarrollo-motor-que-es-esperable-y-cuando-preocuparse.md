**Quick answer.** Babies with Down syndrome reach the same motor milestones as any other baby — sitting, crawling, standing, walking — in the same order, but later and with more variation from child to child. What matters isn't the exact age, it's that the sequence keeps moving forward.

**Why it matters.** Hypotonia (lower muscle tone) and some differences in body structure make motor development slower — on average, 2 to 3 times slower than in children without Down syndrome. Knowing this ahead of time keeps an expected delay from feeling like an alarm every week.

**What the source says.** Studies on motor development in Down syndrome document ranges like these: sitting without support, on average at 9 months (range 6–16 months); standing without support, on average at 18 months (range 12–38 months); walking without support, on average at 24 months (range 16–42 months). That range is wide on purpose — everything inside it counts as typical development for a child with Down syndrome. What specialists recommend watching isn't the age itself, but whether the sequence of milestones keeps moving forward, even slowly.

**What can you do now?** Contact your local early intervention program as soon as possible — you don't need to wait to see a "big" delay to start. Early physical therapy helps build foundational motor skills and can help prevent joint discomfort later on.
